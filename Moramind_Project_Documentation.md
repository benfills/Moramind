# **Moramind Documentation**

## **1\. High-Level Overview**

### **Executive Summary**

- **Project Name:** Moramind
- **Unifying Theme:** Brain health
- **Core Concept:** An application that does not promise behavioral changes or guarantee results (**outcome claim**). Instead, it guarantees a system deliberately designed not to fight the user's natural brain functions (**offering claim**).

### **Project Background**

- **Target Audience:** Teens and adults facing general focus and execution challenges (not exclusively ADHD-specific). Designed for users seeking a gentle, non-judgmental approach to productivity.
- **Core Philosophy:** The central question the homepage answers is not _"what features are there?"_, but rather _"will this app understand my situation?"_. This philosophy drives the "attention-safe" design and the prioritization of low-friction UX.

### **Objectives & Scope**

- **4 Initial Core Features:** Pomodoro, Progress tracking, Scheduler, Habit/addiction recovery tracker.
- **Productivity Categorization:**
  - **Execution:** Pomodoro \+ Scheduler (_"What am I doing NOW?"_ — doing).
  - **Growth:** Progress tracking \+ Habit/addiction recovery (_"How am I changing over time?"_ — reviewing).
  - **Recovery/Rest:** Meditation \+ Sleep tracker (A distinct, separate section).
- **Journaling Scope:** Strict privacy-first approach. Entries are **never** logged to the Moramind server; they are saved locally by default with an optional, user-controlled Google Drive backup.

### **Metrics & Impact**

- **ADHD-Friendly Visual Hierarchy (CTA Design):** Tested and finalized based on the "$300 million button" case study and isolation effect principles to reduce cognitive overload:
  1. **Primary (Hero):** _"Try Now\!"_ — The only solid `--primary-main` (\#3A6194) button, isolating attention to the lowest-friction action.
  2. **Secondary (Navbar):** _"Sign Up"_ — A neutral, outlined charcoal button (predictable and actionable without competing for attention).
  3. **Tertiary (Navbar):** _"Log In"_ — Muted plain text (lowest cognitive weight).
- **"Attention-Safe" Clarification:** While the core app strictly avoids dopamine hooks (except for the conscious trade-off of the "Daily Task" brain game), the landing page is allowed a _controlled, healthy hook_ (the `--primary-main` CTA) to drive conversions without being predatory.
- **Progress Tracking Mechanism (Research-Backed Decision):** Numeric streaks were considered and rejected in favor of a heatmap/calendar visualization. Research on ADHD and habit-tracking indicates streaks can trigger shame spirals and all-or-nothing thinking when broken, and can act as a Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria (RSD) trigger — directly contradicting the "attention-safe," non-judgmental positioning. Heatmaps show activity patterns without a single number that "resets to zero," letting a missed day register as a gap in the pattern rather than a failure.
- **WCAG Contrast Check (Code-Level Color System):**
  - White text on \`--primary-main\` (\#3A6194): **6.33:1** — passes AA for both large/bold text (≥3:1) *and* normal-sized text (≥4.5:1).
  - White text on \`--primary-hover\` (\#2D4C75): **8.74:1** — comfortably passes both thresholds.
  - White text on \`--accent-main\` (\#658231): **4.38:1** — passes large/bold AA, fails normal-text AA (needs ≥4.5:1). Only usable for normal-size text if paired with \`--accent-hover\` instead.
  - White text on \`--accent-hover\` (\#4E6625): **6.46:1** — passes both thresholds.
  - White text on \`--status-error\` (\#B3524D): **4.96:1** — passes both thresholds.
  - White text on \`--status-warn\` (\#CC9933): **2.57:1** — **fails AA even for large/bold text.** \`--status-warn\` should not be used as a solid background with white text in its current form (e.g. a "warning" badge/button). It should either use dark text instead of white, be restricted to non-text uses (borders, icons, subtle backgrounds with dark text on top), or be darkened if a solid white-on-warn treatment is needed anywhere.
  - White text on \`--status-ok\` (\#52806A): **4.51:1** — passes both thresholds, essentially at the normal-text AA boundary.
  - \`--text-muted\` (\#7D7A76) on \`--bg-primary\` (\#F2F0EA): **3.75:1** — fails normal-text AA (≥4.5:1), passes large-text AA. \`--text-muted\` should be reserved for large text, non-essential annotations, or paired with a lighter-weight use case (e.g. placeholder text, timestamps) rather than body copy or anything conveying essential information.
  - \`--text-secondary\` (\#54524F) on \`--bg-primary\` (\#F2F0EA): **6.83:1** — passes both thresholds comfortably; safe for normal body text.
  - \`--text-primary\` (\#242321) on \`--bg-primary\` (\#F2F0EA): **13.78:1** — very high margin, safe everywhere.
  - **Practical implication:** CTA button text must stay large/bold for \`--accent-main\` to remain compliant, but \`--primary-main\` is safe at any text size — reinforcing it as the default choice wherever contrast margin matters. \`--status-warn\` and \`--text-muted\` both carry usage restrictions that should be flagged in any future component using them.

## **4\. Reflection & Future Steps**

### **Challenges & Roadblocks**

- **Navigation Consistency Tension:** Designing a simplified Homepage Navbar (Logo \+ CTA) inherently clashes with WCAG 3.2.3 (Consistent Navigation) because it differs from the 7-icon Dashboard Navbar. This was deemed _defensible by necessity_ for newcomers, but requires careful execution to ensure predictable transitions.
- **Design Translation:** Previous attempts to extract precise data from Figma binary files failed, highlighting the necessity of migrating to Penpot for accessible, measurable UI bounding boxes.

### **Lessons Learned**

- **Trust, but Verify:** The "charcoal CTA incident" reinforced that the AI must visually verify outputs (via screenshots or exact data checks) rather than assuming success based solely on tool execution responses.
- **Nuance in Design Principles:** "Attention-safe" does not mean a 100% absence of visual hooks across the entire product; context matters (marketing conversion vs. daily product use).
- **Message Before Visual ("Anxious/Calm Brain" Incident):** A "Problem → Solution" section was built around a pair of illustrated brain mascots (anxious → calm) intended to replace a text-heavy problem statement for scroll-heavy users. On review, the illustrations communicated a generic emotional state ("this app makes you calm") rather than the specific intended insight ("most productivity apps are built for a general market, not for the people who actually need them"). The visual was removed rather than patched, since the person evaluating it couldn't reconstruct the intended point either — a strong signal it would not land with users skimming quickly. **Takeaway:** when replacing text with a visual to reduce cognitive load, the visual still needs to independently carry the _specific_ point being made, not just a mood adjacent to it. Confirm the message is legible before investing in production quality.
- **JSX Inline vs. Block Text Flow:** Using `flex flex-col` on a heading containing two differently-sized `<span>` children forces them onto permanently separate lines regardless of available width, which broke the "single flowing sentence" reading pattern the Hero headline was specifically designed around (see Key Deliverables). The fix was to let the `<span>` elements flow inline (their default behavior) and constrain wrapping via `max-width` on the parent instead. Relatedly, JSX line breaks between elements can silently eat whitespace between them ("adapt.We" instead of "adapt. We") — an explicit `{" "}` is needed to preserve the space.
- **SVG Sizing Must Be Delegated to CSS, Not Hardcoded:** A hardcoded `width`/`height` attribute directly on an `<svg>` element overrides whatever size its parent container is given via CSS, which caused the Cloud mascot to ignore its responsive wrapper entirely and overflow at small viewport sizes. The general fix pattern: use `width="100%" height="100%"` (plus `preserveAspectRatio`) on the `<svg>`, and control actual sizing via the parent wrapper's CSS (width/aspect-ratio). Also: percentage-based `height` on an element only resolves if its parent has a resolvable height — an `aspect-ratio` on the parent is often necessary to give it one.
- **Raised-Button Shadow/Translate Must Point in Opposite Directions:** For a CSS "raised card/button" effect (element offset via `translate`, with a `box-shadow` simulating the surface it's lifted off of), the shadow's offset direction and the element's translate direction need to be _opposite_ for the illusion to read as "raised" rather than looking like a mismatched, flat drop-shadow. This was caught by rendering both variants side-by-side for visual comparison rather than reasoning about the coordinate signs abstractly — a useful general debugging pattern for any directional/geometric CSS bug.
- **Two Separate Penpot MCP Connection Pathways Exist:** The user has both Claude Desktop (local MCP server via a config file) and the claude.ai web interface (cloud-based Penpot connector) set up to talk to Penpot. These maintain independent sessions/tokens and do not share connection state — a live connection in one does not imply a live connection in the other, even against the same underlying Penpot file (which is itself server-side and unaffected by either client's connection status). When a Penpot tool call fails with "No Penpot instance connected," reconnecting must happen on the specific surface the current conversation is running on.
- **Vite Asset Imports vs. Raw String Paths Are Resolved by Completely Different Mechanisms:** A `src="../file.mp4"` string in JSX is inert text as far as the bundler is concerned — it is sent to the browser as-is, and the _browser_ resolves it at runtime relative to the page's current URL, not relative to the source file's location in the project. This is why it 404'd despite looking like a correct relative path. An `import x from "../assets/file.mp4"`, by contrast, is resolved by _Vite at build time_: Vite copies the file to the build output, generates a correct hashed URL, and substitutes that URL into the imported variable — this always produces a working path regardless of route/page structure. A third option, placing a file in `public/` and referencing it by an absolute root path (e.g. `/file.mp4`), also works because Vite guarantees `public/`'s contents are copied 1:1 to the build root — but this only works if `public/` is a sibling of `src/` (project root level); a `public/` folder nested _inside_ `src/` (as this project currently has) is not auto-served by Vite's static-copy behavior and should eventually be moved or reconciled (flagged, not yet acted on — see Next Steps). General rule going forward: any binary asset (image, video, font) referenced by a literal string path must live in a root-level `public/` folder; any asset referenced via `import` can live anywhere under `src/` and Vite will handle it correctly either way.
- **Flexbox's Default `min-width: auto` Can Silently Defeat `max-width` and `overflow-wrap`:** A flex container's children default to `min-width: auto`, which means a child will refuse to shrink below the width of its longest unbreakable content (e.g. a run of text with no spaces) — even if the child also has an explicit `max-width` and `overflow-wrap: break-word` set. The result is text visibly overflowing a box that appears, from its own computed width, to be correctly constrained. This was fixed in the Hero speech bubble by removing `flex` entirely in favor of plain `text-center` for what was simple centered text — sidestepping the interaction rather than patching it with `min-w-0`. General takeaway: if a flex (or grid) child containing text overflows a `max-width` it shouldn't, suspect the container's default min-width/min-height behavior before assuming the width constraint itself is wrong.
- **A Flex Parent's Default `align-items: stretch` Can Distort an Unrelated Nested Grid's Row Heights:** While building the Progress Tracking heatmap (`Heatblocks`, a flat 28-cell array rendered via `grid grid-cols-7`), grid rows were rendering at 66px tall instead of the intended 28px, creating visible gaps between rows despite no `grid-template-rows`, `gap`, or margin being set anywhere in the chain. Root cause: the grid container was nested inside two ancestor `flex` containers (`dbg1`, `dbg3`) that had a fixed/flowing height and no `align-items` override, so the default `align-items: stretch` forced the grid container itself to stretch to fill the full available height of its flex ancestors (264px in this case). With a grid container height "locked" at 264px but only 4 rows-worth of actual content (112px), the grid's default `align-content: normal` (which behaves like `stretch` for grid tracks) distributed the extra 152px evenly across the 4 row tracks (264 ÷ 4 \= 66px each), instead of leaving it as a single gap after the content. Fixing the grid container's own `width` (`w-fit` instead of `w-1/4`, which had also been distorting column widths via the same kind of ancestor-driven sizing) did not fix the row issue, because width and height are sized independently. The actual fix was adding `content-start` (`align-content: flex-start`, which resolves to grid's `align-content: start`) directly on the grid container — this stops the grid from stretching its row tracks to fill the inherited height, letting rows size to their content (28px) and pushing any leftover space to the bottom instead of distributing it as inter-row gaps. **Key distinction to remember:** `align-items` controls how a container's _direct children_ are sized/positioned; `align-content` controls how a _container's own tracks/lines_ are distributed within its own box. For a single-line flex container, `align-content` has no visible effect (this was initially assumed to be the reason `content-start` "shouldn't" work) — but that flexbox-specific rule does **not** carry over to CSS Grid, where `align-content` is meaningful even for a single group of row tracks. The two layout modes are easy to conflate but follow different rules for this specific property.
